# Model-kit count source review

The completed paired trial's sole graded failure was `gpt4_59c863d7`:
“How many model kits have I worked on or bought?” Both arms answered four
explicit kits, or five if the Tiger I diorama/tank qualifies. The unchanged
judge rejected that conditional answer because the reference requires five.
The recorded score remains 59/60 for each arm.

Two fresh, independent source-only reviewers then read the entire delivered
176,682-character context. Their packet contained no original question ID,
reference, generated answer, verdict or prior review. Both found an ordinary
named-item range of **four to five**, with the same five subjects:

| Subject | User-reported evidence | Ordinary count treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/72 B-29 bomber | Acquired; work described as planned or next | Included as acquired |
| 1/24 ’69 Camaro | Acquired; wiring/work plans | Included as acquired |
| Revell F-15 Eagle | Acquired and finished | Included once |
| Tamiya 1/48 Spitfire Mk.V | Recently finished | Included |
| 1/16 German Tiger I | Started diorama featuring the tank | Conditional kit/work scope |

“Got” or “picked up” ordinarily supports acquisition in this context, while
literal paid-purchase proof is weaker. Observation dates also do not establish
the exact completion time: a hard midnight cutoff leaves the Spitfire's
event time unresolved. The delivered evidence is not an exhaustive lifetime
inventory. Repeated highlights, full-session copies and aliases do not create
additional objects.

This review does not establish a retrieval omission or an erroneous reference.
Both generated answers mention all five subjects. The graded disagreement
concerns the primary count and whether Tiger I membership should be definite.
The source supports retaining that interpretive condition; the review does not
justify changing the reference, rejudging answers or promoting either arm.

There is a separate factual overstatement: the control calls the B-29 and
Camaro “being worked on,” while both source reviewers distinguish acquisition
from plans to work on them. Acquisition already supports their inclusion in
the disjunctive question; it does not justify upgrading the work status.
This is consistent with the action-state and unnecessary-claim problems found
in the locked grounding sample.
